## The brief

> Choose one mycorrhizal phenomenon — from this course or from your own
> reading — give it a diplomatic label of your own, and then put that label
> on trial using the standard this course built in week 1.

This is not a survey of the semester. It is one phenomenon, argued in depth:
what the biology actually shows, what your chosen diplomatic term adds to
that description, and — the part that is actually marked most heavily —
where the term stops describing and starts inventing. Use the week 1
credentials test and the audit format from week 12's Briefing as your
method, not as decoration.

## What you submit

A written piece of 2000–2500 words, or an equivalent artefact (a short
recorded briefing, a designed dossier) if you have discussed the substitution
with the convenor beforehand. Either way, a marker must be able to follow
your argument without you in the room.

The `marking:` block above renders as a holistic statement rather than a
weighted table — this is the one assessment in the course judged as a whole
rather than by separately-scored parts, which matches how a real diplomatic
audit is actually read.
