## Outline

- garlic mustard, invasive across much of North America, produces
  glucosinolate-derived compounds implicated in suppressing native arbuscular
  mycorrhizal fungi in the soil it invades
- native plants that depend on those AM partnerships for nutrient uptake are
  put at a disadvantage exactly where the invader is establishing, which
  helps the invader further and disadvantages the natives it displaces
- this is the clearest one-directional "broken treaty" of the semester: an
  outside party degrading an existing mutualism as a side effect of its own
  establishment strategy, not a negotiated failure between the original two
  parties
- second, smaller case: heavy logging that removes large, well-connected
  trees can leave a common mycorrhizal network without its best-connected
  node — how much this destabilises the wider network is less settled than
  the garlic mustard case, and the lecture says so rather than rounding up
