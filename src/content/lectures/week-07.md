## Outline

- a genet is one genetically distinct fungal individual, which can spread
  through soil far beyond any single visible fruiting body
- where two different genets meet, competitive exclusion and visible
  antagonism at the contact zone is well documented
- self/non-self recognition operates at these boundaries: compatible tissue
  can fuse, incompatible tissue rejects contact — the full genetic mechanism
  is deferred to week 10, where it is the week's main phenomenon rather than
  a supporting one
- root systems from different plants overlap and compete for the same
  nutrient patches, and a mycorrhizal partner can extend or contract that
  competitive reach on the plant's behalf
- "border dispute" fits the contest over a patch of soil; it fits less well
  as a claim that either side recognises the other's claim as legitimate
  rather than simply failing to get past it
