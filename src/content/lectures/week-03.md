## Outline

- David Read's early demonstrations that radiolabelled carbon can move from
  one plant to another through a shared mycorrhizal network, decades before
  the phrase "wood wide web" existed
- Suzanne Simard's field experiments tracing carbon between Douglas-fir and
  paper birch, with measured net transfer toward more shaded seedlings — the
  empirical core of the "mother tree" idea
- what "foreign aid" gets right: transfer can run toward the more
  carbon-limited partner in a connected pair
- what it overclaims: aid implies an intention and a donor that can identify
  need. A mycelium moving carbon down a concentration or sink-strength
  gradient is not choosing a recipient — and how large and how common this
  effect actually is across whole forests remained genuinely contested in the
  literature that followed the popular treatment
- explicit tie back to week 1: this is precisely the kind of claim the
  credentials test exists to slow down before it is repeated as settled fact
