## Outline

- full mycoheterotrophs: Monotropa uniflora (Indian pipe) and its relatives
  have no chlorophyll and take carbon entirely from ectomycorrhizal fungi
  that are also linked to a photosynthesising tree
- the fungus in these cases typically gets nothing back from the
  mycoheterotroph — it keeps supplying the tree as usual, and the
  mycoheterotroph exploits a link that already exists for someone else's
  benefit
- initial mycoheterotrophy: orchid seeds carry almost no food reserve and
  germinate only with fungal carbon support; most orchids switch to
  photosynthesis as adults, some stay dependent for life
- why "espionage" is not an overreach here the way week 3's "aid" was: unlike
  a gradient-driven transfer, there is no reciprocal payment at all, and no
  market mechanism from week 2 that would obviously catch it
- open question carried into weeks 5 and 6: does the network have any way to
  detect and cut this off, or is it close to undetectable free-riding
